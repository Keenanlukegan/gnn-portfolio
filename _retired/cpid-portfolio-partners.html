03 · SCHEDULING / MESSAGINGLIVE Daily WhatsApp Class-Reminder Sender +

An unattended daily sender that reminds every customer of their appointment: built defensively, because messaging apps lie about what they actually sent.

FIELD NOTE

The business problem: appointment-based businesses bleed revenue to no-shows, so someone burns an hour every morning messaging customers by hand, and automate it naively and it quietly double-sends, or logs ‘sent’ for messages that never landed. How he found it: he refused to trust ‘message sent’, and re-reading the screen after every send surfaced that most of a batch logged as SENT had never actually delivered. How he solved it: phone-first opening so it can never message the wrong contact, three concentric dedup guards, and post-send verification that only logs SENT after confirming the message on screen, every rule earned from a real mis-send he caught. Find the silent failure, prove it, engineer it out: the method transfers to any business that messages its customers.

DOCTRINE · WHAT PRODUCTION TAUGHT ME

RULE OF THE ESTATE

Automations lie.

WhatsApp Web's DOM drops its classes overnight. Spreadsheet read-caches go stale. Auth fails with a silent 401 and nothing screams. Every system here treats "success" as a hypothesis and re-verifies it against the source: the rendered bubble, the unread badge, the file on disk.

RULE OF THE HUMANS

No machine speaks to a human unapproved.

Every outbound message drafts to a person first: approve, edit, or reject on Telegram before anything sends. Zero unapproved messages have ever gone out. Not a policy document; an architecture.

RULE OF THE NIGHT

A failure is one glance away.

Dated health reports, provenance-stamped scores, and deterministic watchdogs with no model and nothing that can 401. If expected output goes missing, my phone rings before a student, a parent, or a teacher ever notices.
